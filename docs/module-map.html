The shape of it

Five layers, top to bottom: the app boots, GetX decides which of 3 top-level screens to show, that screen is one of 8 feature modules, modules read from 3 services, services are built from 4 plain data models. The one thing this shape doesn't tell you on its own — flagged directly on the diagram — is that footprint isn't purely "above" home and scanner: it depends on both of them right back.

What actually calls what, at runtime

The diagram above is imports — it says who can reach whom, not who does. Three different screens can each land you on the same Product or Material, and they don't do it by calling each other: all three go through the exact same two lookups, then push a detail screen that logs itself.

Worth knowing before reading either diagram as a strict hierarchy

  • The footprint ↔ home and footprint ↔ scanner cycles are intentional, not accidental — home is embedded as footprint's own tab 0, and the scan button is a real screen push, not a tab.
  • services/catalog is read by everyone, written by one thing. Every screen module reads ProductService/MaterialService directly, but only services/basyx ever calls registerProduct/registerMaterial — once at startup, during the online/offline sync.
  • History logging is self-contained, on purpose. It would be easy to log a view at each of the three entry points instead — Home, Scanner, History — but that's three places to keep in sync instead of one. Putting _logHistory() on the destination screen means a fourth entry point later gets logging for free.
